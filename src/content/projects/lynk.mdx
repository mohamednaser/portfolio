## The Mission

**Lynk** is the infrastructure powering Islamic Murabaha commodity trading in Saudi Arabia. When an institution extends financing, the underlying commodities must be genuinely purchased, owned, and sold. My job is to ensure the systems powering these trades are as legally and structurally sound as the contracts themselves.

## The Architecture & Technical Leadership

As Technical Lead, I own the end-to-end technical execution on the most critical, high-stakes paths of the platform. I direct the engineering team and personally architect the infrastructure that cannot fail. 

This isn't just about moving data—it's about moving millions in capital with zero room for race conditions or state corruption. Here is what I built and scaled to make that happen:

### 1. Immutable Ledger Boundaries
Financial data and operational states are physically separated into different databases. By completely isolating the business logic from the financial truth, we guarantee strict auditability and ensure that a bug in a feature can never corrupt the financial ledger.

### 2. Scaling the Execution Pipeline
To handle massive spikes in concurrent commodity orders, I modeled the entire trade lifecycle as an uncompromising state machine. I re-architected our Laravel queue infrastructure, scaling from 3 to over 120 distributed workers. This allowed us to process thousands of simultaneous trades while safely throttling requests to respect external exchange rate limits (like DMCC and Bursa Malaysia).

### 3. Database Concurrency & Deadlock Elimination
Financial pipelines are unforgiving when it comes to race conditions. Under extreme load, I redesigned our MySQL locking strategies to handle massive concurrency without sacrificing data integrity. I implemented deterministic locking orders and optimized transaction boundaries, entirely eliminating deadlocks while ensuring 100% Sharia compliance across every row.

### 4. First-Class Compliance 
Regulatory constraints are baked into the core architecture, not patched on at the end. Complex requirements, such as Saudi ZATCA e-invoicing and strict multi-tenant data isolation for institutional clients, were treated as foundational primitives during the system design phase.
